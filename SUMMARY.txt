Scope-Aware Security Triage: A Java and Semgrep Provenance Pilot

An empty static-analysis result is ambiguous when the intended files, rules, or revision were not analyzed. We examine this evidence-consumption problem in a small Java provenance pilot. Six authored path-reading fixtures are compiled and executed under four local probes each; four fixtures disclose an outside sentinel in at least one probe. Actual Semgrep 1.183.0 scans use one authored taint rule. We compare a deterministic consumer that treats no finding as clearance with a consumer that first checks revision, file inclusion, source hashes, and required-rule identity. Under partial current-revision analysis, the unqualified consumer clears three files, including two with observed disclosure; scope qualification instead escalates the three unanalysed files. Under a simulated omitted-rule condition, four disclosing files are cleared without qualification, while the qualified consumer escalates all six files. The clean current scan flags five files and has no observed false clearance. The stale-report control over-reports rather than misses findings, so it demonstrates no false-clearance benefit from qualification and exposes an abstention cost. These results establish a reproducible provenance-harness behavior on authored fixtures. They do not establish vulnerability-detector accuracy, security-agent performance, or effectiveness on public repository histories.
